# Attenlo PDF: 36-month growth and profitability
Prepared for Shrey Talwar | 27 September 2026

## Decision summary
This is a conditional growth model, not a forecast or a probability estimate. The Base case reaches approximately 3,638 active paying subscribers and ₹4.94 lakh in monthly operating profit at month 36. The Strong case reaches approximately 16,993 payers and ₹29.28 lakh/month. The Downside fails to reach profitability.

Operating profit includes acquisition, payment/store fees, refunds, paid and free AI usage, subscription tooling, support/tool reserves, core overhead and scaled technical QA/maintenance reserves. It excludes income tax, owner distributions and founder pay (set to zero), with a separate ₹1 lakh/month founder opportunity-cost sensitivity.

The timeline starts when a working product begins paid launch. Prelaunch development duration, existing hardware and existing developer subscriptions are not financed by this launch model.

## Pricing and core economics
The prior proposed prices are retained: India ₹1,999/year or ₹249/month, with an assumed 18% tax included; international $39.99/year or $5.99/month, before local tax. These remain proposals, not user-approved prices. ₹90/USD is a planning convention, not a current exchange-rate quote.

The model uses a 50% web/direct and 50% store sales mix within each geography. Store fee is assumed 15%, subject to eligibility and regional terms. India web uses 2.36%; international web uses 5% plus $0.50. Subscription tooling is reserved at 1% across all sales. Fees on refunded transactions are assumed not recoverable.

Planner usage is 2,500 input tokens and 450 billed output tokens per job, priced at $0.20/$1.20 per million, with 25% retry overhead and a further 10% supplier/FX contingency. Budgeted model cost is ₹0.1287 per job. Paid users average 50 jobs/month; free users use 3 jobs/month. The free-user base is the greater of 500 or 20 active free users per payer.

## What drives growth
Base starts at 1,000 qualified organic prospect visits in month 1. These grow 12% per month in year 1, 8% in year 2 and 6% in year 3. Organic visit-to-first-purchase conversion is 1.5%. Paid conversion is 2%, starting CPC is ₹15, and CPC rises 10% per year. Paid creative is budgeted at 25% of media.

Paid media budgets per month are ₹6,000 in months 1–3, ₹12,000 in months 4–6, ₹25,000 in months 7–12, ₹50,000 in months 13–18, ₹75,000 in months 19–24, ₹1 lakh in months 25–30 and ₹1.25 lakh in months 31–36. Organic content/partner cash budgets add ₹5,000, ₹10,000 and ₹15,000/month by year.

Thus by month 36 Base requires approximately 17,626 qualified organic prospects plus 6,887 paid clicks in the month. These produce approximately 264 organic and 138 paid first purchases, or 402 gross first buyers. It is not enough merely to publish the app on multiple platforms.

Strong uses better organic growth, conversion and renewal and 75% international buyers; it requires about 1,920 gross new buyers/month and ₹5.25 lakh monthly acquisition spending by month 36. These are demanding distribution assumptions, not the default expectation.

## Retention is cohort-based
75% of new Base buyers choose annual plans, 25% monthly. 60% are international buyers. Refunds remove 3% of payment events and associated entitlements.

Annual service lasts 12 months. First annual renewal propensity is 30%; second is 55%, each before modeled refunds. Monthly first, second and later renewal propensities are 60%, 75% and 88%, each before refunds. The model does not pretend monthly churn is a small constant from the first payment.

Downside annual first renewal is 20%; Strong is 45%. RevenueCat's broad mobile subscription benchmarks are a sanity check, not evidence that Attenlo will achieve any renewal level. PDF utilities and web sales may differ.

The large takeaway: obtaining international customers and building a dependable organic channel have more financial leverage in this model than trimming a few paise from an AI request.

## Acquisition must be judged correctly
Base blended cash CAC is approximately ₹497 in year 1, ₹534 in year 2 and ₹487 in year 3. These include media, paid creative and organic-content cash.

Loaded paid-channel CAC is much higher: about ₹938, ₹1,031 and ₹1,134 by year. Organic buyers reduce blended CAC; they do not make a weak paid campaign profitable. Monthly subscribers require their own payback analysis and cannot be treated as annual prepayments.

Base spends ₹3.15 lakh on acquisition in year 1, ₹10.58 lakh in year 2 and ₹18.68 lakh in year 3. These are funded over time in the scenario, not requested as an upfront cheque. Do not confuse opening capital with lifetime campaign spend.

## Capital and cash
The proposed opening ring-fenced cash is ₹1 lakh. In Downside it falls to about ₹1,787 by month 6. Paid acquisition is turned off after month 6, but continuing to maintain the service still loses money. The 36-month loss is an illustration of failure to stop, not a recommended three-year budget.

Base's mechanical end-of-month funding shortfall is only approximately ₹21,262, and Strong's model has an upfront-setup floor of ₹20,000. These are not safe launch budgets: monthly buckets overlook exact payment timing, campaign lags and operational surprises. A ring-fenced ₹1–1.5 lakh Base launch test remains more sensible; Strong calls for extra contingency, not automatic immediate spending.

Annual prepayments give good cash flow before earned profit. Base year 1 earns approximately ₹18,595 total operating profit but ends with approximately ₹7.82 lakh in bank cash, including opening funds and advance receipts. Do not treat that bank balance as first-year profit.

At month 36 Base shows approximately ₹94.66 lakh in bank cash, ₹47.31 lakh of unearned annual revenue, ₹5.06 lakh of payout receivables and ₹51.41 lakh of cumulative operating profit. Reconciliation:
opening cash + cumulative operating profit + unearned revenue - payout receivable = closing cash.

The model separately reserves remaining paid-service costs and three months of current operating/marketing/free-AI cost. A pre-tax retained-profit/liquidity cap is shown, but it is not tax or distribution advice.

## Mature scale targets
At Base geography and channel economics, with a mature annual-only customer base, flat 30% renewal propensity and ₹650 gross cash CAC, approximately:
- 1,000 retained annual payers supports ₹1 lakh/month operating profit.
- 2,430 supports ₹3 lakh/month.
- 3,761 supports ₹5 lakh/month.
- 7,422 supports ₹10 lakh/month.

These include replacement acquisition, overhead and scale QA, not extra growth campaigns. They differ from the growth scenarios, which have monthly-plan cohorts and reinvestment.

## Decision gates
1. First 50 paying customers: verify willingness to pay, first-result quality, refunds and actual model usage.
2. Around 100–200 payers: measure recurring use and separate organic, creator and paid conversion. Scale only channels that earn back their cash cost.
3. By month 6: use the downside cash/traction signal to stop, narrow the audience or revise the offer. Do not defend the spreadsheet against observed results.
4. Before large annual-cohort renewal: retain liquidity, observe recurring workflows and replace assumptions with actual customer behavior.
5. Before claiming ₹10 lakh-plus/month capacity: verify support, parser reliability, app-store compliance, localization and incident response. Solo payroll can stay low, but servicing a large business still takes work.
