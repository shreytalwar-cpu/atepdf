# atepdf: the complete performance marketing plan
**Prepared for Shrey Talwar | 28 September 2026**

## Executive decision
Build a small, measurable marketing operation around the experience, not a large media plan around the word AI.

**Public promise:** Drop your files. Tell atepdf what to do. Get the result.
**Supporting proof:** PDF processing happens on your device. Cloud AI interprets your instructions.
**First paid use case:** Preparing a multi-file document pack: combine files, make scans searchable and reduce the output size for sharing or submission.

The product is broad, but the first experiment is narrow: US-English independent professionals and small-business operators,
using the web application. The primary audience hypothesis is people who repeatedly send document packs,
not occasional visitors seeking a free one-page edit. Use India for a separate founder-led pilot cohort and
later test it as its own paid market with its lower CAC limit. Do not mix cheap Indian clicks and higher-priced
US purchases in one average to claim profitable acquisition.

Start with Meta demonstration ads leading to a matched web landing page and the real workspace.
Do not simultaneously buy Google, Apple, TikTok, LinkedIn and Meta traffic.
Google high-intent search is the second candidate, with a separately approved test. Native acquisition follows
working products and reliable purchase attribution, not a desire to advertise every app store at once.

**Operating aim:** earn 30–50 first payments in a reasonably coherent cohort, learn whether buyers repeat a real job,
and establish contribution after acquisition. These are directional learning milestones, not statistical certainty.

## 2. Launch positioning and customer hypotheses
### The first audience
Independent consultants, office administrators, virtual assistants and small-business operators who already
handle recurring attachments. Target the job with the creative, rather than splitting a small budget into
many fragile job-title and interest audiences.

Pain to test: “I know the result I need, but have to work out which PDF tools to use, in what order.”
Moment to show: several files become one searchable, shareable result, followed by a natural follow-up change.
Buying reason: repeated convenience, batch processing and saved workflows, not access to a fashionable model.

### Audience order
1. Recurring document-pack users: primary hypothesis and first campaign.
2. Professionals handling scans: adjacent OCR angle, only after searchable output is tested.
3. Deadline-driven occasional users: useful top-of-funnel, but monitor repeat usage and monthly versus annual choice.
4. Students/general free-tool users: organic and free-tier audience initially, not the first paid-spend bet.

Do not target based on inferred medical, financial-distress or other sensitive characteristics from documents.
No customer document contents or filename-based segments enter advertising systems.

### Differentiation hierarchy
Lead with fewer decisions and less tool-switching.
Demonstrate the actual workflow in the first few seconds.
Support the promise with local document processing and a simple price.
Use the mascot as branding, not a 10-second opening animation.

Avoid “the first”, “Smallpdf killer”, “100% offline”, “AI sees nothing”, “never uploads any data”,
“every conversion”, “perfect compression” or “guaranteed under 5 MB”.
The typed instruction and limited metadata do reach the cloud after consent.
Say “Your PDFs are processed on your device” and explain the boundary prominently.

## 3. Readiness gates before the first rupee
The ad must promise only an operation enabled in the target platform's verified capability registry.
A linked page must not show an unimplemented OCR or batch feature simply because a design mock-up contains it.

Required:
- A working local merge, accurate searchable OCR when advertised, honest compression and follow-up.
- Independent output checks; original files preserved; cancellation and memory errors handled.
- Mobile browser and desktop task completion; in-app-browser limitations handled honestly.
- A real free result without a required account or surprise output paywall.
- Production-safe cloud gateway, rate limits, bounded retries and spending caps.
- Exact prices, current seller, native/web billing and restoration tested.
- Privacy consent independent from marketing consent; file workspace contains no ad pixels or session replay.
- Verified purchase, refund and entitlement events; synthetic purchase tests excluded from analytics.
- Help, contact, refund/cancellation route and a clear “what happens to my files” explanation.
- 20 observed testers complete the specified task; proposed usability target is 90% without coaching.
- Domain, account, creative rights, copy and bounded spend approved by Shrey.

The numerical usability target is a management gate, not an industry statistic.
Day 1 of paid testing starts after readiness, not on an arbitrary calendar date.

Show the provider's actual localised complete billing price. No currency-by-IP trick that conflicts with checkout.

Free: 3 successfully completed AI jobs per month plus useful local shortcuts.

A multi-step job consumes one allowance, not one per internal engine operation.

### Acquisition path
Ad -> matched landing page -> real workspace -> file import -> instruction/necessary consent -> validated result
-> preview/export -> next real task -> explicit Pro choice or a genuine supported limit -> purchase.

A lightweight sample is optional, not forced. Sample use is not real-user activation.
Preserve the intended instruction when moving from the landing page to the workspace; use a safe preset ID,
not a raw prompt containing customer data in a URL.

### First offer timing
Keep the initial result and export unblocked.
A nonmodal Pro explanation may be available after value; mandatory offer only at a supported new-job limit.
Closing checkout preserves the pending job and files, and never silently starts an extra operation.
Paid users at cap see their next reset, not “Buy Pro” again.

No mandatory email capture to use the sample or first free job.
An optional account enables cross-device entitlement, not document sync.

## 5. Unit economics before channel selection
We carry forward the latest conservative planning assumptions, not unverified supplier quotes:
INR 90/USD planning FX; INR 0.1287 per AI job; 50 paid jobs/month; 20 free active users per payer at 3 jobs/month;
INR 5/month variable support reserve; 3% refund reserve; 1% subscription-tool reserve.
Payment assumptions: 2.36% India web; 5% + USD 0.50 global web; 15% store commission. [C01–C04]

These exclude fixed overhead, income tax and founder compensation.
A job-cost estimate does not validate the particular planner model named in older documents.
Benchmark and price the provider actually selected before going live.

| Route | Annual first-year contribution (INR) | Monthly first-payment contribution (INR) | 30% target: annual CAC | 30% target: 75% annual / 25% monthly blend |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| India web | 1,346 | 177 | 404 | 316 |
| India store | 1,139 | 151 | 342 | 268 |
| Global web | 3,000 | 426 | 900 | 707 |
| Global store | 2,685 | 418 | 806 | 636 |

Annual contribution reserves one year of variable service, monthly contribution only the first month.
Future monthly renewals are deliberately not capitalised into the acquisition limit before observed.
The blended figure is not monthly revenue and is not guaranteed lifetime value.

**First US web target: approximately INR 700 all-in cash CAC at 75% annual mix.
If every buyer chooses annual, the target is approximately INR 900.**
Do not judge mixed-plan campaigns against the annual-only INR 900 target.
India paid traffic has a much tighter first-commitment target, around INR 315 at the same mix.

These are starting risk limits. They can be revised after measured renewal and profitability, not just to make a losing ad look acceptable.
The earlier growth workbook's paid CAC assumption exceeding these targets was a scenario, not permission to overspend.

### Bidding backwards from economics
Cash CAC = (delivered media + nonrecoverable media tax/fees + allocated creative + attributable affiliate costs) / first buyers.
Refund-adjusted CAC is also shown, but do not deduct the same refund loss twice in contribution.
Economic CAC also includes founder time; payroll may be zero, attention is not.

Max delivered-media CPC = (cash CAC target - fixed creative cost per buyer) x click-to-paid conversion / (1 + media tax rate).
For a proportional creative allocation k, max CPC = target CAC x CVR / (1 + media tax + k).
At INR 700 CAC, 2% conversion, 18% media cash-tax assumption and 25% creative burden,
the CPC boundary is roughly INR 9.79. At 3% conversion it is INR 14.69.
These are feasible-price constraints, not predicted US click costs.

Track ex-tax cash receipts, earned revenue, committed service reserve and contribution separately.
A year's upfront payment is not a year's profit available to withdraw.

Exact and phrase matching still include related meaning/intent, so inspect actual search terms. [P01]
Keyword candidates and negatives are supplied without invented search volumes or CPC quotations.

Begin with supported task terms and a visible free allowance / Pro disclosure.
Exclude wrong-intent queries such as signing, editing, PDF-to-Word, crack, API and jobs.
Do not automatically exclude every use of “free”: some prospects can be valid users of our genuine free tier.

## 9. A repeatable creative operation
### Research batch, not endless browsing
Collect 20 candidate references across 5–8 relevant PDF, scanner and approachable productivity apps.
Use Meta Ad Library for ad examples where accessible; use Appllama for onboarding/paywall journeys.
The earlier Appllama study had screen metadata, not a completed pixel audit; refresh actual media before claiming visual review.
Long ad run dates can suggest continued use, but do not establish profitability or reveal competitors' CAC.

For each reference record:
source and capture date; hook; audience/job; visible problem; demonstration; proof; offer; CTA;
landing destination; reuse rights; observed versus inferred facts.
Codex extracts patterns, not competitor footage, reviews or distinctive brand assets.
Do not label a reference “winning” without actual performance evidence.

### Production batch
Create 12 initial briefs = 6 materially distinct concepts x 2 executions.
Only 3 are produced first, then improve the ones users understand.
Use our approved playful palette and subtle mascot. No cartoon-heavy opening, corporate stock video or fake customer persona.
The product footage dominates.

A standard 20-second cut:
0–3s: concrete frustration or desired output.
3–6s: show actual input files.
6–12s: visible one-sentence command and real operation.
12–17s: result with genuine measured file size and searchable output if relevant.
17–20s: one clear CTA and short privacy qualifier.

If elapsed processing is shortened, label the footage as edited/sped up. Do not imply a universal performance guarantee.
Show synthetic documents and real engine output. Never publish a real customer PDF for social proof.

### Initial angles
A. One instruction instead of tool-hopping.
B. Get a multi-file pack ready for submission.
C. Target a file-size limit, with honest trade-offs.
D. Make scanned pages searchable, then actually demonstrate search.
E. Local PDF processing without sending document content to the AI.
F. Repeat a saved recipe and finish recurring admin work.

### Variation rules
A new headline on the same clip is a hook variant, not a new concept.
Label concept, execution, hook and version separately.
Test hook changes without simultaneously changing price, market and landing page.
Use a new language/market only after the first cell is interpretable.
Keep a challenger only when the budget can give it a meaningful diagnostic sample.

## 11. Measurement without betraying the product
Three independent records:
1. Product event ledger: first valid real result, export initiation and repeat job, with no content/filenames/prompts.
2. Purchase ledger: verified first payment, plan, actual price, included tax, fee, refund and entitlement state.
3. Ad-spend ledger: platform charges, delivery spend, tax/fees, creative allocation and campaign IDs.

The purchase ledger is authoritative for money.
The ad platform is an attributed estimate, not the bank.
Do not add Meta, Google, affiliate and Apple reported orders when they refer to the same customer payment.

### Consent and isolation
No ad pixels, tag manager, third-party session replay or advertising SDK in the file workspace.
Use a separate marketing origin or strict isolation so marketing scripts cannot inspect imported files or composer text.
On the marketing site, default nonessential ad measurement off and enable only after appropriate consent.
For Google, the proposed basic consent approach blocks tags until consent, unlike advanced mode's cookieless measurements. [P02,P03]
Marketing permission is not bundled with permission to send an instruction to a cloud model.

Server-side conversion reporting is not a privacy exemption.
Keep consent state with the attribution record; if unavailable, do not fabricate or backfill permission.
Do not hash email addresses merely to call them anonymous.
No cross-device fingerprinting or attempts to bypass ATT for native tracking. [P05]

### Acquisition conversion contract
Preferred initial path: one server-origin first-purchase event from a verified payment webhook, only when matching is permitted.
Use an idempotency key per provider transaction; one first customer payment, not one event per webhook retry.
No purchase event for free activation, pending payment, trial start, restore or subscription renewal.
If browser Purchase is later added, ensure provider-compatible deduplication with the same stable order event key and name.
Validate against current authenticated Meta docs; fresh public API fetch was unavailable. [P09]

Store only the minimal consented campaign identifiers needed for attribution; strip raw query strings from analytics URLs.
Event values use consistent currency and ex-tax first-payment definitions; record any platform display difference.
Do not send a made-up LTV as purchase value. Keep internal service-reserve contribution separate from revenue.

### Reporting windows
Primary operating view: first purchases attributed within a fixed 7-day click window where consented data permits.
Supplement: 30-day acquisition-cohort conversions, because free-to-paid decisions can lag.
Platform view-through reports remain separate; unknown/unattributed stays unknown.
Do not change windows mid-test to make ROAS improve.
Compare cohorts with equal observation time; recent cohorts have incomplete conversion/refund/return windows.

### Event definitions
Landing session, real files imported, cloud-consent outcome, plan accepted, real job validated,
export initiated, repeat real job, offer shown, checkout opened, verified first purchase, refund and renewal.
Ad systems receive at most generic approved funnel/purchase events, not document task content.
Internal operation IDs are diagnostic only; do not infer audiences from them.
Browser save initiation does not prove a file was saved; do not label it confirmed save.

## 12. Scorecard and practical rules
| Stage | Metric definition | What it diagnoses |
|---|---|---|
| Ad attention | 3-second plays / impressions, where available | Whether the opening earns attention; not directly comparable across placements without same denominator |
| Ad intent | Outbound clicks / impressions | Whether viewers want the promised result |
| Landing | Landing sessions / outbound clicks | Loading problems, accidental clicks and measurement gaps |
| Product activation | First validated real-file result / eligible landing sessions | Whether the promise converts into actual use |
| Paid conversion | First verified buyers / attributable prospect sessions | Willingness to pay; use fixed windows |
| CAC | Media cash + creative + attributable partner costs / first buyers | Cost of acquisition; also show nonrefunded-buyer CAC |
| Contribution | Net first-commitment receipts less fees, refund reserve and service reserve, less acquisition | Whether sales pay for delivery and acquisition |
| Repeat use | Buyers completing a distinct second real job within 30 days / observable buyer cohort | Recurring value, not subscription-cash optics |
| Support | Founder minutes + external cash by cohort | Whether a “solo” business is actually manageable |

Do not optimise hook rate instead of paying-customer contribution.
Do not label the founder's 40% hook/3% CTR targets industry standards.
CTR that rises while activation falls may be attracting curiosity rather than buyers.

### Stop and scale policy
Immediate pause recommendation: privacy leak, corrupt output, unavailable checkout, broken claims or spend-control failure.
No performance-model score overrides those conditions.

Zero buyers: after the declared conversion window matures, pause/review when allocated cash spend
reaches around three times the target CAC. This is a risk rule, not statistically conclusive proof.
Include creative and tax allocation. Do not count unspent reserve.

A campaign with a few buyers: report a range and qualitative objections, not a certain winner.
Check at least 20 observed real completions; wrong users or poor output can explain a cheap click.
Use 30–50 buyers across comparable cohorts as a directional scaling gate, with enough time for refund/return checks.
Larger samples may be needed for small A/B differences.

Scale only when cash CAC is within the current mix-adjusted limit, contribution after acquisition is positive,
refunds and support remain acceptable, tracking reconciles and more than one creative works.
Proposed risk guardrail: review refund rates above 5% after a useful sample, versus the model's 3% baseline.
Proposed recurring-value gate: at least 25% of a fully observed early target-user buyer cohort completes a second real job in 30 days.
These are management hypotheses; do not invent an industry benchmark.

Increase budget modestly, e.g. up to 20% at the next scheduled review after at least several stable days,
with adequate mature conversions. This is our cash-risk policy, not a guarantee against platform learning resets.
Track marginal cost per additional buyer as well as blended CAC.
Every increase remains within the approved tranche; no automatic spending authorisation is embedded.

## 14. The 90-day execution sequence
### Weeks 1–2: build evidence, not media spend
20 pilot observations; capture the real workflow; verify consent and purchase events; prepare 3 scripts and 1 landing.
Founder talks to 10 target users; Codex synthesises objections without their documents.
Separate India founder-led testers from US paid acquisition measurement.
Outcome: functioning funnel, rights-cleared assets, exact paid-ready capability and approval checklist.

### Weeks 3–4: diagnostic tranche
Run up to INR 4,000 gross media in the approved US cell, subject to earlier loss controls.
2–3 concepts at a time. Do not change price or onboarding on the same day as the creative test.
Read search/placement/quality signals and actual first purchases; inspect failures and support.
Outcome: a clearer message and identified funnel bottleneck, not a universal CAC conclusion.

### Weeks 4–5: replication tranche
Release up to INR 8,000 only if the first review supports another test.
Repeat the strongest concept with one genuinely different execution.
Fix the identified bottleneck; mark the intervention date and restart comparable measurement.
Outcome: evidence that customers, not just friends or one unusually cheap day, convert.

### Week 6: controlled extension
Up to INR 12,000 gross media if unit economics support it.
No new country and channel simultaneously.
If the first cell fails, keep the money; try a new offer or pause.
Outcome: an interpretable cohort and decision for the next phase.

### Days 43–60: observe repeat value
Review 30-day real-job return, refunds, annual share and founder support load.
Improve saved recipes, imports, truthful expectations and account linking before buying more traffic.
Release additional media only through a NEW written cap.
Optional next-channel test: Google Search OR Apple Ads, not both, if its required product/data is ready.

### Days 61–90: extend a working system
A successful cell may expand in approved steps, with weekly contribution reviews.
Prepare another market or platform using the same proven claim, but evaluate its economics separately.
Maintain a control creative and one challenger; keep a source-to-result research ledger.
If no repeatable conversion system exists, revise audience/offer or stop paid spend. Do not launch more apps to escape the result.

Appllama is research input; it is not the customer analytics backend.

## 16. Retention, reviews and support
Do not create artificial reasons to open a utility daily. Make the next genuine document job easier.
After a second multi-step job, suggest saving a recipe; keep it user-controlled and local.
Offer cross-device entitlement linking without claiming documents sync.
Provide concise help for unsupported files, compression targets, passwords and billing.

Optional lifecycle messages only to users with the required consent:
welcome/help after signup; a recipe tip after actual value; a single later return invitation tied to a real use case.
No fake warnings that local documents will disappear unless the user pays.
No filenames, PDF contents or raw instructions in email or notification payloads.

Ask for reviews via allowed native mechanisms after genuine use, not conditional on a positive feedback answer.
No incentives for reviews and no fabricated social proof.
The first testimonials should describe measured actual experience with permission.
Do not transplant Rohan's revenue screenshot style into atepdf before we have truthful results.

## 17. What success looks like
First: a new prospect understands the promise, produces a valid file and retains trust in the privacy boundary.
Second: some users pay for repeated convenience at a price that covers delivery and acquisition.
Third: a channel works more than once with more than one piece of creative.
Fourth: real repeat usage and renewals replace model assumptions.

The desired outcome is a reliable profitable acquisition process, not a single high-ROAS screenshot.
Launch scope is unchanged: web, Android, iPhone/iPad and Mac, with a working local PDF processor and
cloud instruction interpretation. Growth work must not turn it into a generic chatbot, editor, document-upload service
or complicated dashboard.
